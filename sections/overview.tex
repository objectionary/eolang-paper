\section{Informal Overview}\label{sec:overview}

EO is built around a single primitive --- the \emph{object formation} ---
  and three operations on it: \emph{application}, \emph{dispatch}, and
  \emph{decoration}.
This section walks through each in turn,
  pairing surface EO source with the \(\varphi\)-calculus
  expression~\citep{bugayenko2021eolang} it represents.
The formal mapping is given in \cref{sec:semantics};
  the present section is intended as a reading-on-ramp.

\subsection{Formations and Voids}\label{sec:over:formation}

A formation gathers \emph{named attributes} between square brackets.
An attribute may be a \emph{void} --- a slot with no value attached ---
  or be bound to an expression.

\begin{ffcode}
[a b] > foo
\end{ffcode}

declares an abstract object \ff{foo} with two voids \(a\) and \(b\),
  and translates to the \(\varphi\)-formation
\begin{equation*}
  \mathtt{foo}\;\mapsto\;
    \llbracket\,
      a \mapsto \varnothing,\ b \mapsto \varnothing
    \,\rrbracket.
\end{equation*}

Bound attributes appear inside the formation's body block:

\begin{ffcode}
[] > pair
  42 > x
  "hi" > y
\end{ffcode}

translates to
\begin{equation*}
  \mathtt{pair}\;\mapsto\;
    \llbracket\,
      x \mapsto 42,\ y \mapsto \mathtt{"hi"}
    \,\rrbracket.
\end{equation*}

\subsection{Application}\label{sec:over:application}

Application attaches an expression to a void.
A formation with all voids attached is \emph{closed}; a formation that
  still carries voids after partial application is again abstract.
EO offers two notations.
\emph{Horizontal} application places the head and arguments on one line;
  \emph{vertical} application puts each argument on a deeper-indent line.

\begin{ffcode}
foo 1 2 > closed-foo            // horizontal
\end{ffcode}

and

\begin{ffcode}
foo > closed-foo                // vertical (same meaning)
  1
  2
\end{ffcode}

both translate to
\begin{equation*}
  \mathtt{closed\text{-}foo}\;\mapsto\;
    \mathtt{foo}\,(a \mapsto 1,\ b \mapsto 2).
\end{equation*}

An argument may carry an explicit binding (\cref{sec:syntax:binding}):
  \ff{foo 1:a 2:b} binds \ff{a} and \ff{b} by name rather than position.
The two notations are interchangeable up to whitespace;
  the well-formedness rules of \cref{sec:wf:bindings} require an argument
  group to use bindings uniformly or not at all.

\subsection{Dispatch}\label{sec:over:dispatch}

A dispatch retrieves the expression attached to a named attribute.
EO writes a dispatch in dot notation; the left side is the scope being
  inspected and the right side is the attribute name.

\begin{ffcode}
pair.x
\end{ffcode}

translates to
\begin{equation*}
  \mathtt{pair}.x.
\end{equation*}

Dispatches chain freely:
\begin{ffcode}
io.stdout
\end{ffcode}

translates to \(\mathtt{io}.\mathtt{stdout}\),
  reading \enquote{the \ff{stdout} attribute of the \ff{io} object}.

The locators \ff{Q}, \ff{\$}, \ff{@}, and \ff{\textasciicircum} stand for
  the root namespace \(\Phi\), the current scope \(\xi\), the decoratee
  \(\varphi\), and the parent \(\rho\), respectively.
For example, \ff{Q.io.stdout} translates to \(\Phi.\mathtt{io}.\mathtt{stdout}\):
  start at the root namespace, then dispatch into \ff{io}, then \ff{stdout}.

\subsection{Decoration via \texorpdfstring{\(\varphi\)}{phi}}\label{sec:over:decoration}

A formation that binds the special attribute \ff{@} \emph{decorates} the
  object attached to that \(\varphi\) attribute.
A dispatch on the decorating formation that finds no local match falls
  through to the same dispatch on the decoratee.
Decoration is EO's only mechanism for behavioural reuse;
  there is no class hierarchy and no implementation inheritance.

\begin{ffcode}
[] > app
  Q.io.stdout > @
    "Hello, world!\n"
\end{ffcode}

The object \ff{app} decorates a copy of \ff{Q.io.stdout} applied to a
  greeting string.
A dispatch like \ff{app.write} (for any attribute of \ff{stdout} that
  \ff{app} does not redefine) reduces to the same dispatch on the
  decoratee.
The \(\varphi\)-form is
\begin{equation*}
  \mathtt{app}\;\mapsto\;
    \llbracket\,
      \varphi \mapsto \Phi.\mathtt{io}.\mathtt{stdout}\,(\text{\ff{"Hello, world!\textbackslash n"}})
    \,\rrbracket.
\end{equation*}

\subsection{Atoms}\label{sec:over:atom}

Some operations cannot be expressed inside EO itself --- for example,
  writing a byte to standard output, or adding two integers represented as
  byte sequences.
An \emph{atom} is a formation whose behaviour is supplied by a
  host-language function.
The signature suffix \ff{/sig} on the name declares the return type and
  marks the formation as an atom.

\begin{ffcode}
[a b] > plus /number
\end{ffcode}

declares an atom \ff{plus} with two voids \(a\) and \(b\) and a return
  signature \(\mathtt{number}\).
Its \(\varphi\)-form attaches a \(\lambda\)-asset
  (a host-language function reference) to the formation:
\begin{equation*}
  \mathtt{plus}\;\mapsto\;
    \llbracket\,
      a \mapsto \varnothing,\ b \mapsto \varnothing,\
      \lambda \mapsto \mathtt{Plus}
    \,\rrbracket.
\end{equation*}

The body of an atom contains only test attributes (\cref{sec:wf:atoms});
  the operational meaning lives in the \(\lambda\)-asset.

\subsection{Method Chains}\label{sec:over:chain}

A chain of dispatches reads top-to-bottom in vertical notation:

\begin{ffcode}
tmpdir
.tmpfile
.deleted
.open > @
  "r"
\end{ffcode}

translates to
\begin{equation*}
  \mathtt{tmpdir}.\mathtt{tmpfile}.\mathtt{deleted}.\mathtt{open}\,(\alpha_0 \mapsto \mathtt{"r"}),
\end{equation*}
where the name on the last \ff{.method} line (here \ff{@}, indicating
  decoration) is the name of the whole expression
  (\cref{sec:wf:naming}).
Intermediate links may carry their own optional names.

\subsection{Reversed Dispatch}\label{sec:over:reversed}

Some method names read more naturally in prefix position than in
  postfix.
EO offers a \emph{reversed dispatch}: the line begins with the method name
  followed by a trailing dot, and the receiver is the first argument.

\begin{ffcode}
if. cond then else
\end{ffcode}

translates to \(\mathtt{cond}.\mathtt{if}\,(\alpha_0 \mapsto \mathtt{then},\ \alpha_1 \mapsto \mathtt{else})\) ---
  exactly the same as the postfix \ff{cond.if then else} would denote.
A vertical form spreads the receiver and arguments across deeper-indent
  lines (\cref{sec:syntax:reversed}).

\subsection{Compact Tuple}\label{sec:over:tuple}

A tuple of values is built by the \ff{*} construct.
A line of the form \ff{head *N} partitions its vertical children:
  the first \(N\) are direct positional arguments of the head, and the
  remaining children are collected into a single
  \(\Phi.\mathtt{tuple}\) argument appended at the end.

\begin{ffcode}
sprintf *1 > greeting
  "Hello, %s!"
  "Jeffrey"
  "extra"
\end{ffcode}

binds \ff{"Hello, \%s!"} as the first positional argument of \ff{sprintf};
  the remaining children \ff{"Jeffrey"} and \ff{"extra"} fold into a
  tuple argument.
The \(\varphi\)-translation is
\begin{equation*}
  \mathtt{greeting}\;\mapsto\;
    \mathtt{sprintf}\,(
      \alpha_0 \mapsto \mathtt{"Hello, \%s!"},\
      \alpha_1 \mapsto \Phi.\mathtt{tuple}(\mathtt{"Jeffrey"},\ \mathtt{"extra"})
    ).
\end{equation*}

A bare \ff{*} in head position is itself a \(\Phi.\mathtt{tuple}\)
  constructor over its arguments.

\subsection{A Program}\label{sec:over:program}

A complete EO program is a top-level formation that decorates the object
  it should evaluate to.
Reducing the program to a sequence of bytes ---
  the operation called \emph{dataization} (\cref{sec:sem:atoms}) ---
  follows the chain of decorations and dispatches until a \(\Delta\)-asset
  is reached and its bytes are returned.

\begin{ffcode}
+package org.eolang.examples

# Greets Jeff.
[] > app
  Q.io.stdout > @
    Q.tt.sprintf
      "Hello, %s!"
      * "Jeffrey"
\end{ffcode}

The program declares a single object \ff{app} whose decoratee is a copy of
  \ff{Q.io.stdout} applied to the formatted greeting.
Dataizing \ff{app} reduces the dispatch chain, evaluates the atom backing
  \ff{stdout}, and writes the bytes of \enquote{Hello, Jeffrey!} to the
  process's standard output.
The complete \(\varphi\)-translation of this program is left to
  \cref{sec:semantics}; the present overview has established what each
  surface construct denotes.
